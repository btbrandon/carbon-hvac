Our proposal asked whether energy savings come at the expense of unreasonable comfort. §8 answers that for the average occupant. This section asks who chose the trade-off, who bears it, and whether we reported it fairly.

**Who sets the weights, and who pays.** The reward is a multi-attribute utility function, and its weights encode a value judgement, not physics. At λ_K = 5 it treats one degree-hour of discomfort as equal to 5 kg of CO₂, and that exchange rate decides the result. On non-holiday days PPO's violations range from 45% to 0.9% of occupied steps as λ_K goes from 0.5 to 50 (Table 8.5). In a building, the facility manager would set the weight while the occupants bear its effects. We therefore report the full Pareto front rather than one tuned point. We also favour a comfort *constraint*, which is why our extension treats comfort that way (§8.7). "Violations below 1%" is a satisficing criterion [1]: occupants can agree to it and audit it in a way they cannot a weight. A further step would be to learn the trade-off from occupants' stated preferences rather than fix it by hand [2]. The 23–26 °C band [3] is itself a choice, and thermal preference varies between people [4, 5]. Our hinge penalty is also linear, although people weigh a loss of comfort more heavily than an equal gain [6]. Comfort also has to be treated as a distribution rather than a mean. PPO's average discomfort is small (0.076 °C·h/day over all test days), but its violations fall at the edges of the occupied day (§8.6), with 53% between 17:00 and 18:30 [CHECK: recompute for tuned PPO; not in `results/tables/`]. Late leavers above all, and early arrivals, therefore bear the cost of the savings. We therefore report violation rate and maximum deviation alongside the mean (§7.1).

**Specification gaming and proxies.** An agent optimises the reward it is given, not the intent behind it [7]. The standard mitigation for this reward over-optimisation is to evaluate independently of the signal the agent can influence. Our reward therefore charges discomfort on *true* occupancy, not the sensor reading (§4.1). Otherwise a policy could profit whenever the sensor wrongly reads "empty". Outside simulation the reward would have to come from the sensor, so sensor reliability becomes a safety issue. That matters here because 72% of PPO's carbon saving comes from reading the sensor on three holidays (§8.1). A sensor stuck at "empty" on a workday would leave people without cooling. §8.5 will test random sensor errors; the stuck sensor is a harsher case that we have not tested. The carbon signal is also a proxy: our CI profiles are UK-shaped and synthetic, so the carbon figures are not Singapore savings.

**Honest baselines.** How strong the baseline is decides most of the reported gain. Our first comparison, default-setting PPO against a carbon heuristic with only two settings tuned, showed an 11.3% carbon saving (§6.4). Tuned PPO saves 3.7% against the tuned rule and 0.9% against a rule with a holiday calendar (Table 8.3). Only the last answers the question a building owner would ask. The first number would have anchored readers [8], and us, on a gain that tuning the rule removes; checking the result day by day is what exposed the holiday effect. We report all three and tune the rules and PPO under one protocol (§6.4). DQN was not tuned, which we note as a limitation (§8.8).

**Transparency and oversight.** The tuned rule fits in one sentence ("cool to 25.9 °C while people are present") and anyone can check it. PPO's policy is close to it but can only be described after the fact. Because the rule with a calendar captures nearly all of PPO's benefit at almost no discomfort, the rule is the responsible choice for this building. RL justifies its lower explainability only where there is a real lever to learn, such as storage or many zones (§10). In that setting humans must stay in command: a hard comfort constraint, a fallback to the rule, an occupant override, and monitoring of violations by time of day. The facility manager who sets the constraint remains accountable for its effects.
